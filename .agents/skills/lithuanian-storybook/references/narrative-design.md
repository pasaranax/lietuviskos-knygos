# External fiction as a source of narrative possibilities

Read this when proposed episodes keep sharing a dramatic skeleton. Read the relevant actual story or a coherent scene, rather than relying on a remembered synopsis. The sources below were read in full as short stories on 2026-10-09; this is a starting set, not a required reading list or a style model.

- [O. Henry, After Twenty Years](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/2776/2776-h/2776-h.htm): a routine encounter gains a different significance through what the participants know and choose to disclose. Examine how an ordinary action establishes a detail whose function becomes apparent later.
- [Saki, The Open Window](https://opentextbc.ca/englishliterature/chapter/hector-hugh-munro-saki/): speakers pursue their own purposes and interpret the same arrival differently. Examine how dialogue changes another person’s reading of visible events instead of acting as neutral world documentation.
- [James Joyce, Araby](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/2814/2814-h/2814-h.htm): anticipation, mundane delays and an encounter change the value of the original goal. Examine how waiting and travel themselves carry pressure and how a scene can culminate in changed understanding.

These observations are editorial readings of the linked texts. They are not rules that every episode needs concealed identity, an unreliable speaker or disappointment. Compare differing approaches, then return to the particular characters, stakes and canon of the current book.

Ask what the reader expects at each point, what a character does that changes those expectations, and which earlier detail makes that change coherent. Notice which actions receive space and which intervals are compressed. Distinguish a practical sequence of service steps from the dramatic sequence the reader experiences. Reject variants that only swap the object, sacrifice or decoration of an existing episode.

External material should broaden the available choices. Borrowing a plot turn, conflict structure or event sequence and giving it this world’s circumstances is an authorized creative method. Adapt the roles, motives, stakes and causal links to these characters and their established magic; a borrowed turn should change what they do rather than appear solely to surprise. Combine or transform sources when useful and write new prose for the book. Avoid repeating a construction already used in this book or turning a source’s technique into another obligatory template.
