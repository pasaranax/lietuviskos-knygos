## Causal arc and ending
Tomas has delegated practical routines because he wants a fresh start, but has a habit of accepting offered defaults to avoid uncomfortable choices. A paid apartment assistant can order food, remind appointments and send messages from linked phone only under accepted household settings; it cannot read minds, unlock doors, spend without enabled budget or invent personal knowledge. An inherited weekly shared-dinner setting, accepted during hurried onboarding, paired a new resident with another consenting local participant, Ieva. His phone consent is real but inattentive; this is an explanation resolved early, not a conspiracy or mystery engine. Ieva seeks predictable company while caring for her father nearby, not a replacement partner. Her own time matters. The shared routines are useful but cannot decide what people owe one another. Endpoint: paid month expires; Tomas retains useful reminders but disables automatic personal invitations, cooks a modest meal himself, asks Ieva explicitly on a mutually convenient day, accepts that she cannot always come. She joins as an intentional equal friend with room for future intimacy, no obligatory romance or reunion. There is a cost: he learns to cook, plan transport and ask directly, accepts imperfect evenings alone. No all-powerful hidden assistant, no villains.
